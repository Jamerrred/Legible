## Connector feasibility: what can each source actually do for a 24-business pilot?

### Takeaway
Nine of the 22 sources are usable in a pilot without waiting on a platform's approval (Google Calendar, Outlook, Calendly, Acuity, Square, Shopify, WooCommerce, Wix, Stripe). The real blockers are Google Business Profile (a 60-day-old verified profile plus a manual approval with no stated timeline) and Meta (Advanced Access needs App Review and Business Verification), and Toast, Mindbody, Vagaro, Fresha and Yelp are gated, priced or closed enough that a pilot should take their data manually.

**Google app-review blockers.**
- Sensitive and restricted scopes need verification; non-sensitive scopes do not ([Google](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13463073)). Submission needs a demo video of the full OAuth flow plus scope justification ([requirements](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13464321)).
- Testing status allows up to 100 test users, and test authorizations and refresh tokens lapse seven days after consent ([audience](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945)).
- Unverified apps show a warning and are capped at 100 new users in total over the project's lifetime, which cannot be reset ([audience](https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945)).

**Meta app-review blockers.**
- Development mode can only request permissions from role users ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/development/build-and-test/app-modes)).
- Tech Providers serving multiple businesses need Advanced Access via App Review ([app review](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/app-review)).
- Testers are added under App Roles; the Instagram account must be public; multiple can be added **[S]** ([setup](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/create-an-instagram-app/)). An older forum thread shows acceptance through Instagram's Authorized Applications page and that "Tester" differs from "Instagram Tester", which may be out of date **[S][3P]** ([forum](https://developers.facebook.com/community/threads/2645731442173067/)).
- A person can hold developer or admin roles on at most 15 apps not tied to a verified business **[S]** ([roles](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/development/build-and-test/app-roles)).
- Manual export exists: Accounts Center > Export your information, HTML or JSON. That article covers personal profiles and does not cover Pages ([Facebook help](https://www.facebook.com/help/212802592074644)).

### Inferences
- A brand-new Legible without an aged Google profile cannot pass the GBP gate itself. Apply using a founder's or friendly pilot business's profile that is 60+ days old. Until approved, fill GBP data by interview. Nothing indicates the 60-day rule can be waived.
- For Google Calendar in a 24-owner pilot, publishing the OAuth consent screen as "in production, unverified" looks better than Testing status: it accepts a warning screen and burns up to 24 of the 100 lifetime slots, but avoids seven-day re-consent, since the docs tie seven-day expiry to Testing only. Confirm this behaviour in a test project. Use `calendar.freebusy` plus a separate `calendar.events` grant only when booking is on.
- For Meta, running Development mode with each owner as an Instagram Tester is workable technically for 24 businesses, but it adds a role-invite step per owner and is intended for development and testing, so using it with real client accounts is a policy risk rather than a cleared path. Submit App Review in parallel so a rejection or delay is visible early.
- The MCP servers now offered by Calendly, Square, Stripe, Shopify and Wix change the build: Legible can sometimes broker to them rather than re-implement each API. Note these mostly assume a user-authorized client, so they help read and act for the owner, not expose a public consumer endpoint, with Wix and Shopify storefront endpoints as exceptions.
- Recommended pilot order: website crawl and document upload first, then Google Calendar, Square, Calendly, Shopify Storefront, Wix, WooCommerce, Stripe. Add Instagram and GBP as soon as approvals allow. Leave Toast, Mindbody, Vagaro, Fresha, Etsy, Yelp, TikTok, WhatsApp and Gmail to manual intake.

- Whether Shopify's UCP Catalog MCP answers for every store regardless of plan or the Agentic Storefronts setting: one forum answer says manifests are generated when Agentic Storefronts is enabled **[S][3P]** ([forum](https://community.shopify.dev/t/how-to-get-well-known-ucp-on-shopify-plus-with-a-custom-headless-storefront/28470)); not confirmed.

## Website ingestion: which tools crawl a small-business site and extract facts, and at what cost?

### Takeaway
Crawling is effectively a solved and very cheap problem at pilot scale (cents per site). The harder work is structured facts: only Firecrawl documents deterministic schema.org extraction, so keep raw HTML, run a JSON-LD parser, and use a vision LLM for image and PDF menus.

### Inferences
- Cost per site, assuming 30 pages (my assumption, not a measured small-business average):
  - Firecrawl: 30 credits markdown plus JSON on 5 pages = 50 credits, about $0.19 at Hobby or $0.05 at Standard rates. All pages as JSON is 150 credits, $0.57 or $0.15.
  - Cloudflare: at an assumed 5 s per page, 2.5 min or under $0.01 at overage. 24 sites refreshed weekly is about 4 browser hours, inside the 10 included on the $5/month Workers plan, excluding Workers AI.
  - Jina: at about 2,500 tokens per 10 kB page ([Anthropic's estimate](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/web-fetch-tool)), 75,000 tokens per site, so 10M free tokens covers about five passes of 24 sites.
- Cost per menu page, assuming about 1,860 input tokens and 1,500 output tokens: Haiku 5.5 about $0.001, Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite about $0.001, Sonnet 5.5 about $0.019. That is below the third-party $0.01 to $0.10 range for cheap models. All 24 businesses at 4 menu pages each costs under $2 even on Sonnet 5.5.
- Suggested pipeline: Firecrawl or Cloudflare to crawl, keep raw HTML, run `extruct` before any markdown conversion, LLM-normalize visible text into the Legible schema, vision LLM for PDFs and image menus, then owner review. Cost is immaterial; extraction errors in prices and hours are the actual risk.

### Gaps
- No 2026 benchmark found of menu-specific structured extraction across Gemini, Claude and GPT. Run a small test on a few dozen real menus scored against hand labels.
- Jina per-token paid price, Firecrawl self-host licensing, and whether Crawl4AI's result object retains JSON-LD by default were not confirmed.
- Real average page counts for small-business sites were not sourced.

## Fallback: what does non-API onboarding look like?

### Takeaway
Manual intake should be the default path rather than an edge case, since Tier C sources and blocked Meta or Google connections all land there. Extraction costs are negligible, and owner review time is the actual limit.

### Cited Findings
- **CSV.** Shopify exports products to CSV from admin ([Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/import-export/export-products)). Etsy sellers can download active listings as CSV, including titles, descriptions, prices, quantities, tags and image URLs ([Etsy](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000343508-How-to-Download-Your-Shop-Data)).
- **PDF.** Claude reads text, pictures, charts and tables in PDFs, up to 600 pages per request (100 under a 1M context window) ([PDF](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/pdf-support)). Firecrawl parses PDFs at 1 credit per page ([Firecrawl](https://www.firecrawl.dev/pricing)).
- **Screenshot or photo.** Claude accepts images up to 8000x8000 px, and a typical phone-size image resizes to roughly 1,560 tokens on default handling ([vision](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)).
- **Social export.** Meta export gives HTML or JSON; the article covers personal profiles, not Pages ([Facebook help](https://www.facebook.com/help/212802592074644)).

### Inferences
- A workable order of intake: (1) paste website URL and auto-crawl; (2) upload anything: menu PDF, price list, CSV, screenshots of booking pages or Instagram highlights; (3) short chat interview to fill gaps the crawl could not answer (hours exceptions, deposit rules, service area, what agents may do); (4) optional voice note transcribed and treated as interview input; (5) owner approves a one-screen preview, field by field, for prices and hours.
- Reuse public URLs rather than integrating when the only need is a hand-off. A Calendly link, Fresha or Vagaro booking page, Stripe payment link or WhatsApp click-to-chat link can be stored as "where to book or pay" with no API at all. This gives agents a usable action for every Tier C source.
- Freshness is the real weakness of manual intake. Plan a scheduled re-crawl plus a monthly confirmation prompt to the owner, and show `lastVerified` per field on the agent page so agents and owners can see staleness.

### Gaps
- Speech-to-text pricing for voice notes was not captured.
- No sourced data on owner completion rates for interview-style versus form-style onboarding.
- Square, Wix, Toast and Google Takeout export paths were not checked.

- Owner step is a generated, per-platform copy-paste with a verification button that fetches their homepage and confirms the link is live. Footer link first, head tag second, llms.txt line third.
- Native Wix and Shopify endpoints reduce Legible's value on those platforms for catalog facts. Legible's edge there is merging data the platform does not hold, such as calendars, Instagram or owner policies. Non-platform service businesses are the stronger fit.
- A Legible URL proves nothing about ownership. A link back from the owner's own domain, plus `sameAs` from the page to the owner's domain and social profiles, is the cheapest claim of authenticity. A DNS TXT record may still help Legible verify ownership, separately from discovery.

- Whether Wix head code needs a paid plan: the article says connected domain and is silent on plan.

## Discovery conventions: how does an agent on a website find a linked machine-readable endpoint?

### Takeaway
There is no adopted standard. No `rel="mcp"` exists; MCP's own Server Card extension chose `/.well-known/ai-catalog.json` and rejected DNS and header-based discovery, WebMCP is in-browser only, and the most concrete page-level conventions are llms.txt's `rel="describedby"` and `rel="alternate"` and RFC 9727's `rel="api-catalog"`, with no evidence agents act on any of them.

### Cited Findings

| Mechanism | Current status | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| `<link rel="mcp">` | No spec found **[S]** | Unregistered. `rel="webmcp"` seen in one tool, also unregistered **[S][3P]** ([cf-webmcp](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fbasgr%2Fcf-webmcp)) |
| `rel="service"` for MCP | Proposal closed 10 Jul 2025 | Issue #561 opened 21 May 2025, redirected to registry discussions, no SEP linked ([GitHub](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/modelcontextprotocol/issues/561)) |
| `rel="service-desc"` / `service-doc` | Registered web-linking relations for API descriptions, not MCP-specific **[S][3P]** | Points to OpenAPI-style descriptions ([summary](https://specification.website/spec/agent-readiness/link-headers.md)) |
| `rel="api-catalog"` | **RFC 9727, Standards Track, June 2025** | `/.well-known/api-catalog`, Link header, or an anchor in page body ([RFC 9727](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9727.html)) |
| `rel="describedby"`, `rel="alternate" type="text/markdown"` | llms.txt v2, modified 10 Aug 2026, self-described "proposal" | `describedby` points a page to its llms.txt; `alternate` to Markdown. Declines RFC 8615 well-known ([llmstxt.org](https://llmstxt.org/)) |

- Agent readiness is near-zero in the wild. In Cloudflare's scan of the top ~200,000 domains (April 2026): robots.txt 78%, Content Signals 4%, Markdown negotiation 3.9%, Server Cards and API Catalogs on fewer than 15 sites ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/agent-readiness/)).
- Actual fetch behaviour: Claude's web fetch returns text, does not render JavaScript, honours robots rules, and fetches only URLs seen earlier in the conversation, including in previous fetch results ([Claude](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/web-fetch-tool)).
- JSON-LD visibility is doubtful. One test reports 6 of 7 platforms could not retrieve schema markup when asked, with only Gemini returning it, and visible text mattering most **[S][3P]** ([summary](https://blckalpaca.at/en/knowledge-base/seo-geo/geo-generative-engine-optimization/schema-markup-for-ai-search-engines)). Another reports invalid JSON-LD still surfacing, suggesting models read it as text **[S][3P]** ([summary](https://www.pulse.bot/ai/news/report-chatgpt-perplexity-treat-structured-data-as-text-on-a-page-f52c73bf-9225-48f4-a54b-a964bbed1a31)).
- llms.txt autonomous fetching: a vendor's nine-site test reported none **[S][3P]** ([vendor test](https://www.ovclawyermarketing.com/images/LLM_TXT_Research_Paper_Final.pdf)); other logs show occasional generic requests with no ranking or citation lift **[S][3P]** ([Promptwatch](https://promptwatch.com/blog/llms-txt-has-no-impact-on-ai-search-and-geo)). No independent or peer-reviewed study was found.

### Inferences
- Publish several weak signals rather than betting on one. On each agent page: visible text and a visible link; `<link rel="alternate" type="text/markdown">`; `<link rel="describedby">` to a per-business llms.txt; `<link rel="api-catalog">` or a catalog URL; a server card at `/b/slug/mcp/server-card`; JSON-LD with facts repeated in visible text. On Legible's apex: `/.well-known/ai-catalog.json` listing tenants' cards, matching SEP-2127's SaaS example.
- Avoid inventing `rel="mcp"`: nothing would consume it, though it costs little if a future convention adopts that name.
- WebMCP is relevant only for a site running script in a user's browser with a consuming browser agent. It cannot serve the "agent pings a link" model, and it requires script on the owner's site.
- Treat "agent follows a link from a page" as a hypothesis, and instrument it: log user agents and referrers on every agent page and MCP endpoint in the pilot. That data would be unusual and useful.
- A visible "for AI agents" sentence may look like prompt injection to a careful agent, so word it as a plain description ("Structured business profile") rather than an instruction.

### Gaps
- No primary statement from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google or Perplexity found saying their agents follow `rel="alternate"`, `describedby`, `service-desc` or llms.txt from a landed page. Claude's fetch doc does not say whether `<head>` link URLs survive text extraction. A controlled test with a unique token per mechanism would answer it directly.
- Chrome's own WebMCP origin-trial status was not opened, so dates above rest on third-party blogs that disagree.
- Consumer ChatGPT and Claude rules for adding custom connectors were not captured (one help URL returned 404, search exhausted). Only the developer APIs were verified.

## Multi-tenant MCP hosting and auth: can one deployment serve many business endpoints?

### Takeaway
Yes, and it became much easier with MCP 2026-07-28, which removed sessions and the initialize handshake, so one stateless deployment can route by host or path. Cloudflare Workers is the cheapest and simplest fit, and public read-only access without auth is permitted by the spec.

### Cited Findings
- MCP current version is **2026-07-28** ([versioning](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/versioning)). It removes protocol sessions and `Mcp-Session-Id`, removes the handshake, requires `server/discover`, adds `Mcp-Method` and `Mcp-Name` headers on Streamable HTTP POST, and adds `ttlMs` and `cacheScope` to list and read results. List results no longer vary per connection ([changelog](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/changelog)).
- Path-scoped servers are explicitly valid: `https://mcp.example.com/server/mcp` is a canonical URI "when path component is necessary to identify individual MCP server" ([auth spec](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/draft/basic/authorization)).
- **Public read.** "Authorization is OPTIONAL for MCP implementations" ([auth spec](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/draft/basic/authorization)). OpenAI's docs say auth is optional and cite a server needing none ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/tools-connectors-mcp)). Claude's `authorization_token` is optional, and the server must be publicly exposed over HTTP ([Claude](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/mcp-connector)).
- **Authenticated actions.** If protected, servers MUST implement RFC 9728 Protected Resource Metadata and validate token audience per RFC 8707, tokens MUST use the `Authorization` header, and clients SHOULD use Client ID Metadata Documents ([auth spec](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/draft/basic/authorization)). Dynamic Client Registration is deprecated as of 2026-07-28 ([changelog](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/changelog)).
- **Client lag.** Claude's connector docs still link the 2025-11-25 auth spec and mention SSE ([Claude](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/mcp-connector)), and Cloudflare's transport page links the 2025-06-18 spec ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/agents/model-context-protocol/transport/)). The spec documents backward compatibility with initialization-based versions ([versioning](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/versioning)).

| Host | MCP approach | Multi-tenant domains | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cloudflare Workers** | `createMcpHandler` recommended, stateless, Streamable HTTP only. `McpAgent` deprecated. Keep state in DO, D1, KV or R2 ([transport](https://developers.cloudflare.com/agents/model-context-protocol/transport/)). OAuth provider library and Stytch, Auth0, WorkOS, Descope demos; authless not described ([auth](https://developers.cloudflare.com/agents/model-context-protocol/authorization/)) | Cloudflare for SaaS: 100 included on Free/Pro/Business, $0.10 after, 50,000 max ([SaaS](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/cloudflare-for-saas/plans/)) | Free: 100,000 requests/day. Paid $5/month: 10M requests, then $0.30/M, no egress fees ([pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/)) |
| **Vercel** | `mcp-handler` v2, drops HTTP+SSE and Redis config; `withMcpAuth` verifies tokens but does not issue them; docs updated 18 Sep 2026 ([Vercel MCP](https://vercel.com/docs/mcp/deploy-mcp-servers-to-vercel)) | One deployment serves all tenants ([platforms](https://vercel.com/docs/platforms)). Hobby 50 domains per project, Pro unlimited with soft 100,000 limit, 100 additions/hour, wildcard needs Vercel DNS or delegated validation ([limits](https://vercel.com/docs/platforms/multi-tenant-platforms/limits)) | Not captured for functions |

### Inferences
- Recommended: one Cloudflare Worker or Vercel function routing `/b/{slug}/mcp` or `{slug}.legible.ai/mcp`, loading a tenant's JSON from KV, D1 or Postgres. Expect about $5/month for 24 businesses. Railway's 20-domain cap and lack of a free custom-domain tier make it a weaker pick if offering `agents.business.com`.
- Set `cacheScope: "public"` with a sensible `ttlMs` for tenant read results, since these are public facts. Note that a shared endpoint serving many tenants must key caches by tenant path.
- Split the surface. Public read tools need no auth. For booking and other actions, the awkward question is whose identity is being authenticated: a consumer agent has no account with Legible. Pragmatic pilot option: no OAuth for consumers; unauthenticated `request_booking` that holds nothing until the owner or their booking system confirms, or simply return the business's own booking link. Rate-limit by IP and tool name, which the new `Mcp-Name` header makes easy at the edge.
- If OAuth is needed later, per-tenant path URIs let tokens be audience-bound to one business under RFC 8707. Legible then needs its own authorization server or a provider; `mcp-handler` does not include one, and Cloudflare's library does.
- Support both current and handshake-based protocol versions for now, given docs on client side still reference older specs.

### Gaps
- Live interoperability of consumer clients with stateless 2026-07-28 servers was not tested.
- Whether Workers `createMcpHandler` without the OAuth wrapper is officially endorsed for anonymous servers: the docs do not address it directly; it follows from the wrapper being described as the addition for authenticated servers.
- Vercel function pricing at this load was not captured.

## Instagram and Facebook bio links: can they carry a machine-readable hint, and do agents follow them?

### Takeaway
No machine-readable hint is possible beyond the URL string itself, and Instagram's robots rules disallow crawling by default, so a compliant agent will not read a bio to find the link. Bio links are for humans; an agent gets to Legible through the website, search, Google Business Profile, or a user pasting the link.

### Cited Findings
- Instagram's `robots.txt` has `User-agent: *` with `Disallow: /`, and names GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, PerplexityBot, Applebot-Extended and Amazonbot with full disallow. `ChatGPT-User` is unlisted, so the wildcard covers it ([robots](https://www.instagram.com/robots.txt), fetched 3 Nov 2026).
- Claude's web fetch lists `robots.txt` among reasons a URL is blocked with `url_not_allowed` ([Claude](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/web-fetch-tool)).
- Bio links pass through Instagram's `l.instagram.com` redirector **[S][3P]** ([Unilink](https://www.unilink.us/blog/instagram-bio-link-not-working)), and Instagram or Facebook appends query strings **[S][3P]** ([forum](https://forum.textpattern.com/viewtopic.php?pid=342458)). Both are low-quality sources.
- GBP's `websiteUri` is writable by the Business Information API and may be "preferably one specific to this location" ([Location](https://developers.google.com/my-business/reference/businessinformation/rest/v1/accounts.locations)). GBP also has a Place Actions API at 300 QPM ([limits](https://developers.google.com/my-business/content/limits)); its reference was not opened.

### Inferences
- Instagram controls the markup, so `rel` attributes and head tags are impossible. The only hint is a readable URL such as `legible.ai/b/slug` or a bio line like "Book or ask via AI: legible.ai/b/slug".
- Better social and listing surfaces, in order: (1) the business's own website link and footer; (2) Google Business Profile website or action links, since Google's data is more accessible to agents than Instagram's; (3) Facebook Page website field; (4) link-in-bio set to the agent page itself so that human and agent see the same URL, avoiding a separate Linktree layer; (5) `sameAs` from the agent page to the social profiles so identity resolves in the other direction.
- Browser-driving agents operating a logged-in user's session could read a bio link, unlike server-side fetchers. Whether they do so is unverified.

### Gaps
- I found no evidence, for or against, that any agent actually follows Instagram or Facebook bio links. The search returned only link-tool marketing. A pilot-side test via referrers and a unique bio-only URL per business would answer it.
- `facebook.com/robots.txt`, TikTok profile crawling, and Meta's own agent or Business Agent discovery behaviours were not checked.
- Meta Business Agent is priced on the WhatsApp pricing page ([pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)), but how it relates to third-party resources was not researched and may matter as a competitor or channel.
